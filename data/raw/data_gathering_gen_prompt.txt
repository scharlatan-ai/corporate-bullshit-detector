# Task: Build a Social-Media-Focused Dataset for the Corporate Bullshit Detector™

Create an English-language dataset for an experimental AI/ML project called **Corporate Bullshit Detector™**.

The detector is primarily intended for **corporate and professional social-media communication, especially LinkedIn**.

Typical target texts include:

- corporate LinkedIn posts
- founder / CEO / executive LinkedIn posts
- startup announcements
- product-launch posts
- AI / technology hype posts
- consulting thought leadership
- professional “visionary” posts
- company updates
- corporate marketing posts

A smaller number of press releases and corporate website texts should remain in the dataset as comparison / out-of-domain examples.

Do **not** generate classifier probabilities or bullshit scores.

---

## Dataset Size

Create exactly **180 samples**.

Target composition:

- **126 synthetic samples (~70%)**
- **54 real samples (~30%)**

The dataset should be strongly focused on social-media-style communication.

Approximately **75–80% of all samples should be LinkedIn/social-media style**.

### Suggested distribution

Synthetic:

- ~100 LinkedIn / professional social-media style samples
- ~26 press-release, corporate announcement, website, or related business communication samples

Real:

- ~40 authentic LinkedIn or other professional/social-media posts
- ~14 authentic corporate website, press-release, startup announcement, or similar texts

LinkedIn should be the primary real social-media source.

Other public social-media platforms such as X may be used when useful and reliably accessible, but do not depend on them.

---

## Real Samples

Use publicly accessible English-language posts and business communication.

Prioritize real material from:

- companies
- startups
- founders
- CEOs
- executives
- consultants
- technology leaders
- professional corporate accounts

Especially seek realistic examples covering:

- AI hype
- product announcements
- corporate transformation
- leadership
- innovation
- sustainability
- future-of-work claims
- consulting thought leadership
- funding / growth announcements
- ordinary factual company updates

### Authenticity Requirement

Every sample marked `real` must contain **authentic original wording from the public source**.

Do not:

- paraphrase
- summarize
- rewrite
- recreate
- imitate the source

If a real post is too long, shorten it by selecting a relevant excerpt.

You may remove irrelevant sections or select a shorter consecutive passage, but **do not rewrite wording within the selected excerpt**.

Never generate synthetic text and attribute it to a real person or company.

---

## Synthetic Samples

Synthetic samples should resemble **realistic LinkedIn and professional corporate communication**.

Do not make them obvious satire.

They should plausibly look like something a:

- startup founder
- CEO
- executive
- consultant
- corporate communications team
- AI company
- large enterprise

might genuinely post.

Use synthetic samples particularly to control combinations of the four linguistic dimensions we want to study:

1. Buzzword-heavy language
2. Vague / formulaic corporate language
3. Grandiose or implausible claims
4. Concrete and specific information

These dimensions must **not always correlate**.

Generate examples such as:

- very high buzzwords + high concrete information
- high buzzwords + low grandiosity
- high grandiosity + few buzzwords
- vague but modest corporate language
- highly concrete product announcements
- marketing-heavy but informative posts
- visionary but still plausible posts
- factual low-hype business updates
- bland, vague thought leadership
- moderate corporate hype
- concrete posts with measurable results
- strong claims supported by real-looking specifics

Some examples may be strongly exaggerated, especially around AI, technology, futurism, transformation, AGI, automation, productivity, disruption, or societal impact.

However, extreme samples should remain **realistically plausible**. Think aggressive real-world startup/executive rhetoric, not parody-level nonsense.

---

## Difficulty and Balance

The dataset should not make classification artificially easy.

Do not create a simple split where:

- synthetic = bullshit
- real = factual
- AI = bullshit
- marketing = bullshit
- buzzwords = bullshit

Instead, deliberately create difficult and mixed examples.

Approximately **25–30% of the entire dataset should be clearly substantive, concrete, and relatively low-hype**.

Include meaningful numbers of ambiguous or mixed cases.

Examples:

- a LinkedIn product announcement containing genuine technical details but also heavy marketing language
- a corporate post with almost no buzzwords but extremely vague claims
- a highly ambitious founder post containing measurable evidence
- a dry factual post with little marketing language
- a consulting post that sounds polished but communicates very little
- an AI announcement with buzzwords but also specific benchmarks or product details

---

## Text Length

Most samples should resemble normal professional social-media posts.

Typical length:

**30–150 words**

Include some shorter posts and a smaller number of longer examples up to approximately **200–250 words**.

Avoid long-form articles, reports, or detailed blog posts.

## Main CSV Schema

Create:

`corporate_bullshit_raw_dataset.csv`

with exactly these columns and this order:

```text
id
synthetic_or_real
text
source_type
domain
archetype
```

### Fields

`id`

Sequential identifier:

```text
CBS001
CBS002
...
CBS180
```

`synthetic_or_real`

Exactly:

```text
synthetic
```

or

```text
real
```

`text`

The sample itself.

For real rows, the wording must come from the original public source without paraphrasing.

`source_type`

Examples:

```text
linkedin_post
x_post
press_release
product_announcement
corporate_update
thought_leadership
executive_statement
corporate_website
```

`domain`

Examples:

```text
ai_tech
software
finance
energy
consulting
healthcare
retail
manufacturing
logistics
hr_leadership
```

`archetype`

One descriptive construction category.

## Real-Source Verification File

Create a second private file:

`corporate_bullshit_real_sources.csv`

with:

```text
id
source_url
source_title
company_or_author
platform
```

This should contain one row for every real sample.

For LinkedIn samples, preserve the public LinkedIn post URL whenever possible.

This file is for private verification and will not necessarily be published with the project.

---

## Real-Sample Verification

For every row marked `real`, verify:

1. The source is genuinely public.
2. The sample actually appears in that source.
3. The wording has not been paraphrased.
4. Any shortening preserves the original wording.
5. The correct URL is recorded in the source file.
6. The sample is genuinely social-media content when `source_type` says `linkedin_post` or `x_post`.
7. Synthetic material has not accidentally been classified as real.

Do not label a paraphrased summary as `real`.

---

## Quality Checks

Before delivering the files, verify:

1. Exactly **180 rows** exist.
2. Approximately **126 are synthetic and 54 are real**.
3. Approximately **75–80% of the entire dataset is social-media style**.
4. Approximately **40 of the 54 real samples come from LinkedIn or comparable professional/social media**.
5. No exact duplicates exist.
6. Avoid obvious near-duplicates and template repetition.
7. Multiple industries and company types are represented.
8. Roughly **25–30% are substantive / concrete / relatively low-hype**.
9. Buzzwords, vagueness, grandiosity, and concrete information occur in deliberately different combinations.
10. Real samples have authentic source wording.
11. CSV escaping works correctly for commas, quotation marks, apostrophes, emojis, and line breaks.
12. Do not generate classifier scores, probabilities, overall bullshit labels, or ground-truth judgments.
